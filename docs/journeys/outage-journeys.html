Communication service journeys

Each diagram follows one thing a person or a system does, through only the parts it touches. Time runs down the page. Every sticky is one part of the system, and every slip inside it is one step. Switch the wording to Technical for endpoints, class names and settings.

  • Arrows are numbered in order: 1, 2, 3.
  • A step carries the number of the arrow that set it off. Arrow 4 lands on step 4a, the sticky carries on with 4b, and 4b sends arrow 5.
  • A thin grey dashed arrow is an answer coming back. The step it lands on keeps the same number, for example 5a answers and 5b carries on.
  • A blue arrow is news reaching everyone who listens (the live stream), not a request to one part.
  • A step marked ▶ is where a person, a system or the clock starts something.

Publish an outage and watch it land

Sam publishes an outage from the screen; every dependent system sees it within a second, and sees it leave when Sam resolves it.

POST /v1/communications through the client-id filter, a Mongo write plus an event-log append, SSE fan-out of created, then POST …/retire and a retired frame. Proven by e2e/tests/02-f90-publish-live-resolve.spec.ts (F-90).

The same journey, step by step
  1. StartOutage screensSam picks the tenant, fills in Title, Severity and Affected services and presses Publish → 1 to The service
  2. 1aThe serviceChecks who is calling and that the notice makes sense
  3. 1bThe serviceWrites the notice down → 2 to The record book
  4. 2aThe record bookKeeps the notice and adds a line to the log, in order → answer 3 to The service
  5. 3aThe serviceAnswers Sam: published → answer 4 to Outage screens
  6. 4aOutage screensShows the confirmation and offers View in feed and Publish another
  7. 3bThe serviceTells everyone who is listening → 5 to Dependent systems
  8. 5aDependent systemsThe outage appears on every screen within a second
  9. StartOutage screensLater Sam opens the feed, presses Resolve and confirms ("Resolve as web-oncall? It leaves tenant acme's feed at once and cannot be reopened."); Escape would cancel → 6 to The service
  10. 6aThe serviceMarks the notice resolved and notes who did it → 7 to The record book
  11. 7aThe record bookRecords the change → answer 8 to The service
  12. 8aThe serviceConfirms to Sam and tells everyone → 9 to Dependent systems
  13. 9aDependent systemsThe card leaves every screen; a reload of the feed no longer lists it

  3. 1bcommunication-servicePublishingUseCase saves the document and appends a created event with the next global seq → 2 to MongoDB
  4. 2aMongoDBcommunications insert; communication_events gets seq from the counter (unique per log) → answer 3 to communication-service

  6. 4afrontend (React SPA)onPublished renders the confirmation card; onViewFeed(communication) switches tab and filter and moves focus to the results
  7. 3bcommunication-servicethe event-log tailer (polls every 500 ms) fans the created frame out to every open stream of that tenant → 5 to consumers
  8. 5aconsumersSSE id: &lt;seq&gt;, event: created; the UI's inFeed() applies the same visibility rule as GET /v1/feed
  9. Startfrontend (React SPA)POST /v1/communications/{id}/retire with {actor, note}; Escape in the confirmation cancels and returns focus to Resolve → 6 to communication-service

Schedule a maintenance window

Sam announces Saturday's maintenance two days ahead. Consumers see it as upcoming, the clock turns it active on time and closes it when the window ends; until it starts Sam can withdraw it.

A MAINTENANCE notice with startsAt/endsAt is stored SCHEDULED, listed by the feed inside the 7-day upcoming window, and moved SCHEDULED → ACTIVE → EXPIRED by StatusSweepUseCase with activated/expired frames.

  7. StartThe clockSaturday 02:00: the clock finds the window has started → 5 to The record book
  8. 5aThe record bookThe notice is now active; the change is on the log → answer 6 to The service
  9. 6aThe serviceTells everyone the maintenance is on → 7 to Dependent systems
  10. 7aDependent systemsThe card turns active everywhere
  11. StartThe clockAt the end time the clock closes it → 8 to The record book
  12. 8aThe record bookMarked finished → answer 9 to The service
  13. 9aThe serviceTells everyone it is over → 10 to Dependent systems
  14. 10aDependent systemsThe card leaves; the record stays readable by its link

A system publishes through the API, safely twice

A deployment tool announces an incident with its own reference. A retry does not create a second notice; a follow-up for the same incident needs a new reference; an update says which version it is editing.

externalRef is a permanent idempotency key per tenant (unique index): same body → the same notice, a second notice → 409 naming the holder, with the first follow-up value still free in suggestedExternalRef; PUT carries expectedVersion and gets 409 on a stale version (optimistic @Version); misspelled fields are one 400 that names every one of them (unknown fields 'sevrity', 'tittle'; known fields: …).

The same journey, step by step
  1. StartA publishing systemThe tool posts an incident with reference INC-1 and its own name in the caller header → 1 to The service
  2. 1aThe serviceStores it under that reference → 2 to The record book
  3. 2aThe record bookOne notice, one reference → answer 3 to The service
  4. 3aThe serviceAnswers: created → answer 4 to A publishing system
  5. 4aA publishing systemThe network blipped, so the tool sends exactly the same request again → 5 to The service
  6. 5aThe serviceRecognises the reference and gives back the same notice instead of a duplicate
  7. 5bThe serviceAnswers with the same notice → answer 6 to A publishing system
  8. 6aA publishing systemA week later the incident reopens; the tool tries INC-1 again with a new title → 7 to The service
  9. 7aThe serviceRefuses and explains: the reference is taken for good; INC-1-2 is still free → answer 8 to A publishing system
  10. 8aA publishing systemThe tool publishes the follow-up under INC-1-2, then edits the first notice's text → 9 to The service
  11. 9aThe serviceEdits only if nobody changed it meanwhile → 10 to The record book
  12. 10aThe record bookThe edit and its history line → answer 11 to The service
  13. 11aThe serviceTells everyone about the new notice and the edit → 12 to Dependent systems
  14. 12aDependent systemsBoth notices are on every screen, the first with its new wording
  1. StartAPI clientPOST /v1/communications with externalRef: "INC-1", X-Client-Id: deploy-tool → 1 to communication-service
  2. 1acommunication-serviceinsert; unique index external_ref_idx on (tenantId, externalRef) → 2 to MongoDB
  3. 2aMongoDBdocument version: 0 → answer 3 to communication-service
  4. 3acommunication-service201 + body → answer 4 to API client
  5. 4aAPI clientidentical body, same externalRef → 5 to communication-service
  6. 5acommunication-servicelookup by (tenantId, externalRef); the retry is answered with the existing notice, nothing is written
  7. 5bcommunication-servicesame id, same version → answer 6 to API client
  8. 6aAPI clientdifferent body, same externalRef → 7 to communication-service
  9. 7acommunication-service409 {"error": "externalRef 'INC-1' is already used by notice &lt;id&gt; in tenant acme. externalRef is a permanent idempotency key: … (for example INC-1-2, which is still free).", "suggestedExternalRef": "INC-1-2"}; past 50 taken follow-ups it says so and suggests none (documented in /v3/api-docs) → answer 8 to API client
  10. 8aAPI clientPOST with externalRef: "INC-1-2" → 201; then PUT /v1/communications/{id} with expectedVersion: 0 → 9 to communication-service
  11. 9acommunication-service@Version check: 409 "expectedVersion 0 does not match current version 1" when stale; 400 "expectedVersion is required: …" when missing → 10 to MongoDB
  12. 10aMongoDBversion +1, updated event → answer 11 to communication-service
  13. 11acommunication-servicecreated and updated frames → 12 to consumers
  14. 12aconsumersclients apply frames by (id, version), so a repeated frame is harmless

A dependent system reads the feed and stays current

Team B's app loads what is live for its tenant once, then keeps a line open for changes. When the line drops it picks up exactly where it left off.

GET /v1/feed for the first page, then SSE GET /v1/feed/stream?tenantId= with X-Client-Id (or ?clientId=), : connected at once, keepalive every 15 s, Last-Event-ID or ?lastEventId= (with both, the larger wins) replays from the event log, a snapshot frame when the id is too old (log keeps 24 h).

  6. 5aThe serviceOpens the line at once and sends the current picture first → answer 6 to Dependent systems
  7. 6aDependent systemsFrom now on every change arrives as it happens; a quiet line still ticks every 15 s
  8. StartDependent systemsThe line drops (proxy restart). The app reopens it saying the last number it saw → 7 to The service
  9. 7aThe serviceReplays what was missed since that number, then continues live → 8 to The record book
  10. 8aThe record bookThe missed lines of the log → answer 9 to The service
  11. 9aThe serviceSends the missed changes → answer 10 to Dependent systems
  12. 10aDependent systemsNothing was lost; the app did not have to reload the whole list
  1. StartconsumersGET /v1/feed?tenantId=acme&amp;service=payments-api with X-Client-Id: team-b-app → 1 to communication-service

The sidecar notices the team's app is down

Priya's app starts with the sidecar beside it. When the app's database fails, the sidecar sees it within seconds, the service opens an outage with the detail, and closes it when the app recovers. Nobody on Priya's team touched a keyboard.

Discovery via /proc/net/tcp, GET /actuator/health every 15 s (retry after 2 s), UNKNOWN until the app first answers UP (grace 5 min), POST /v1/health/reports on a change, after every probe while DOWN or DEGRADED, and at the 30 s heartbeat; per-pod health_instances, HealthRollup, debounce failureThreshold 2 → OUTAGE, recoveryThreshold 2 → retired. Proven by e2e/tests/03-f91-sidecar-auto-notice.spec.ts (F-91).

  3. 2aThe team's appStill starting: not healthy yet → answer 3 to Health sidecar
  4. 3aHealth sidecarSays "don't know yet" rather than "down" while the app boots (up to 5 minutes) → 4 to Health desk
  5. 4aHealth deskRegisters the app automatically the first time it hears from it
  6. StartThe team's appThe app is up → answer 5 to Health sidecar
  7. 5aHealth sidecarReports UP; from now on it only speaks when something changes or every 30 s → 6 to Health desk
  8. 6aHealth deskRolls the pods of the service up: healthy
  9. StartThe team's appThe app's database fails: the app says it is down because of db → answer 7 to Health sidecar
  10. 7aHealth sidecarChecks again two seconds later so a hiccup does not count, then reports DOWN with the detail, and keeps reporting after every probe while it stays down → 8 to Health desk
  11. 8aHealth deskWaits for a second DOWN before acting, then opens an outage: "fake-app is down" with the failing part named, written down like any other notice → 9 to Dependent systems
  12. 9aDependent systemsTeam B sees "fake-app is down" about 30 s after the database failed, with the reason
  13. StartThe team's appThe database is back; the app is healthy again → answer 10 to Health sidecar
  14. 10aHealth sidecarReports UP → 11 to Health desk
  15. 11aHealth deskAfter a second healthy report it closes the outage by itself → 12 to Dependent systems

  2. 1ahealth-sidecar (Go)lists listening ports from /proc/net/tcp, tries /actuator/health lowest port first, keeps the first target that answers → 2 to app container
  3. 2aapp container503 {"status":"DOWN"} or connection refused during boot → answer 3 to health-sidecar (Go)

The app crashes, or the pod disappears

Three ways a pod can stop answering, and what each one looks like: the app process dies, the cluster deletes the pod cleanly, or the pod vanishes without a word.

Port vanished → DOWN "app is not listening (last target …)" from the first failed probe; SIGTERM → final report with instance.leaving: true (pod leaves the rollup at once); no reports for staleAfterSeconds (90 s) → the sweep marks the pod stale, DOWN when no live pod is left.

The same journey, step by step
  1. StartThe team's appThe app process crashes; nothing listens on its port any more → answer 1 to Health sidecar
  2. 1aHealth sidecarReports DOWN at once: "app is not listening", and says so after every probe until it is back → 2 to Health desk
  3. 2aHealth deskSecond DOWN → outage opened; when the app comes back and answers UP twice → closed → 3 to Dependent systems
  4. 3aDependent systems"fake-app is down: app is not listening" on every screen
  5. StartThe clusterA deploy or a scale-down deletes the pod cleanly → 4 to Health sidecar
  6. 4aHealth sidecarSends one last message: "this pod is leaving" → 5 to Health desk
  7. 5aHealth deskTakes the pod out of the count straight away, so a scale-down never looks like an outage → 6 to Dependent systems
  8. 6aDependent systemsNothing changes for consumers unless the last pod left while down
  9. StartThe clusterA node dies: the pod vanishes with no goodbye → 7 to Health desk
  10. 7aHealth deskAfter 90 s of silence the desk treats the pod as gone; if no healthy pod is left, the service is down → 8 to Dependent systems
  11. 8aDependent systemsThe outage opens about a minute and a half after the pod last reported; it closes once a new pod reports healthy
  1. Startapp containerport gone from /proc/net/tcp; the pinned/discovered target refuses connections → answer 1 to health-sidecar (Go)
  2. 1ahealth-sidecar (Go)notListening() on the probe that finds the port gone; rediscovery runs while it is DOWN; /status.lastTarget keeps the old target → 2 to health slice
  3. 2ahealth slicesame debounce as any DOWN; crash-looping containers therefore show as one outage, not a flap → 3 to consumers
  4. 3aconsumersOUTAGE card, publisher health-sidecar
  5. StartOpenShift / kubeletkubelet sends SIGTERM to every container, sidecar included → 4 to health-sidecar (Go)
  6. 4ahealth-sidecar (Go)final POST /v1/health/reports with instance.leaving: true, then exit 0 (leaving report sent) → 5 to health slice
  7. 5ahealth sliceinstance marked left; rollup over the remaining live pods; scale-to-zero leaves nothing stale → 6 to consumers
  8. 6aconsumersno frame unless the rollup status changed
  9. StartOpenShift / kubeletno SIGTERM delivered; the sidecar's last report ages → 7 to health slice
  10. 7ahealth sliceHealthSweepUseCase every 5 s: a pod not heard from for 90 s (instance-stale-after-seconds) is no longer live; when the whole service has been silent that long the sweep observes DOWN "1 pod has not reported since <UTC> (<pod>)"; safety valve: paused while more than 30 % of the monitors (and at least 2) are silent together, and for 5 minutes after the service itself starts → 8 to consumers
  11. 8aconsumersstale at 90 s, two sweeps 5 s apart reach failureThreshold 2 → OUTAGE; retired after recoveryThreshold UP reports from the new pod

Several pods and a rolling deploy

Priya's app runs three pods. One pod can be unhealthy without a notice; a rolling deploy never opens one; two unhealthy pods out of three open a health notice, and an outage follows only when no pod is left healthy.

Per-pod records in health_instances; HealthRollup over live pods: all UP → UP; some UP → UP while ≥ minHealthyPercent (50 %) are UP and none is DEGRADED (UP with a failing component), otherwise DEGRADED; none UP → DOWN; UNKNOWN (starting) pods are live but not tallied; leaving pods are out at once. Native sidecars start before and stop after the app.

The same journey, step by step
  1. StartThe clusterThree pods run, each with its sidecar → 1 to Health sidecar
  2. 1aHealth sidecarEach sidecar reports its own pod → 2 to Health desk
  3. 2aHealth deskCounts pods, not messages: 3 of 3 healthy
  4. StartThe clusterA rolling deploy replaces the pods one at a time → 3 to Health sidecar
  5. 3aHealth sidecarNew pods say "starting", old pods say "leaving" → 4 to Health desk
  6. 4aHealth deskNever below the healthy share during the deploy: no notice
  7. StartHealth sidecarLater one pod's app hangs → 5 to Health desk
  8. 5aHealth deskOne of three unhealthy is still a healthy service: nothing is published, because the cluster already keeps callers away from the bad pod
  9. StartHealth sidecarA second pod fails too → 6 to Health desk
  10. 6aHealth deskBelow half healthy, twice in a row: a health notice opens (degraded, not down), naming the pods → 7 to Dependent systems
  11. 7aDependent systems"fake-app is degraded" with the pod names in the detail; if the last pod fails too, an outage takes its place
  1. StartOpenShift / kubelet3 × (app + health-sidecar); CS_SERVICE from app.kubernetes.io/name, so all three feed one monitor → 1 to health-sidecar (Go)
  2. 1ahealth-sidecar (Go)instance.id = pod name; 3 rows under the same (tenant, service) monitor → 2 to health slice
  3. 2ahealth slicerollup UP, message 3 of 3 pods UP; GET /v1/health/monitors/{id}/instances lists them
  4. StartOpenShift / kubeletnew pod: sidecar UNKNOWN during grace; old pod: SIGTERM → leaving → 3 to health-sidecar (Go)
  5. 3ahealth-sidecar (Go)UNKNOWN reports (not tallied) and leaving: true reports (removed at once) → 4 to health slice
  6. 4ahealth slicelive pods 2–3, UP share stays ≥ 50 %; no DOWN rollup, no debounce started
  7. Starthealth-sidecar (Go)one pod DOWN, two UP → 5 to health slice
